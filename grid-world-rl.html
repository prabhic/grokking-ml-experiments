Learning

0.20

The step size of every update: new Q = old Q + α × surprise. At 1.00 the latest experience overwrites everything (jumpy, trusts luck); at 0.05 Q creeps slowly but averages out randomness. 0.20 = keep 80% of what I believed, blend in 20% of what just happened.

0.90

How much tomorrow's reward is worth today. A reward n steps away counts as γⁿ of itself: at 0.90 the +100 chest is still worth ≈35 seen from ten steps out; at 0.50 it's worth ≈0.1 — effectively invisible from across the board.

0.20

Before every single move the robot rolls a random number: below ε → go in a random direction (explore), otherwise → follow the best-known Q (exploit). Exploring looks dumb in the moment, but it's the only way new routes ever get discovered.

Explore a lot while young and ignorant, settle down once the Q table is trustworthy.

Rewards

+100

Paid once, on the move that lands on the chest — and the episode ends there. This is the only good news in the whole world.

−50

Paid on the move that lands on the dragon; the episode ends there too. Standing next to it costs nothing — the red halo around it is learned fear, not an actual penalty.

−1

Charged on every move. This is the only thing that makes short paths better than long ones — set it to 0 and the robot has no reason to hurry.

What one step does, inside

  1. Look. The robot's state is just the square it stands on — a single number 0–19. It sees nothing else: no map, no treasure direction.
  2. Roll. A random number is compared with ε. Below ε → move in a random direction (explore). Otherwise → take the direction with the highest Q on this square (exploit).
  3. The world answers. One new square and one number, the reward: the step cost, plus +100 if it landed on the chest or −50 on the dragon. That is the entire feedback.
  4. Write. Exactly one cell of the Q table shifts a fraction α toward "reward + γ × best Q of the new square". Nothing else in memory changes.
  5. Repeat until treasure, dragon, or 300 steps. That whole run is one episode. Turn on "Narrate the math" and press Watch to see steps 1–4 with real numbers.

The words on this page

state · s
Which square the robot is on. Here that's the whole world as the robot experiences it.
action · a
Up, right, down, or left — four choices in every square.
Q(s,a)
The robot's estimate: "total future reward if I take action a from square s and play well afterwards." Its entire brain is this 20×4 table — the coloured triangles.
reward · r
The single number the world pays for one move. Feedback, not instructions.
return
All the rewards of one episode added up — the score in the chart.
episode
One run from START until treasure, dragon, or giving up at 300 steps.
policy
The rule "in every square, follow the biggest Q." Drawn as the black arrows.
ε-greedy
The explore-vs-exploit coin flip made before every single move.
surprise · TD error
What a step actually turned out to be worth minus what Q predicted. Learning is just shrinking the surprise.

One update per step

Every move produces a reward and a new square. That's the whole loop — the robot never sees the map, only the last transition.

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α · [ r + γ · maxa' Q(s',a') − Q(s,a) ]

The bracket is the surprise: what the step actually paid, minus what Q predicted it would pay. Q is the retained state; the episode loop is the scan cycle.

Why the arrows spread

Only two squares carry real reward. Everything else gets value by contagion: a square next to a good square becomes good, one step discounted by γ, and that credit walks outward episode by episode.

That's why value appears near the treasure first and reaches the robot last — and why the dragon stains its neighbours red even though standing beside it costs nothing.

Try this

  1. Train 1000, then drop γ to 0.5 and train again. The far squares go flat — the robot can no longer see the treasure from across the board.
  2. Set cost per step to 0. Watch the returns stop improving even while the policy still finds the chest.
  3. Put the dragon directly beside the treasure. The best path gets longer on purpose.
  4. Set ε to 0 before training. The robot commits to the first thing that worked and never finds better.
  5. Forget everything, then 🎮 Drive it yourself straight to the chest two or three times. Toggle Best path: your route is now its policy — it learned it from watching you, without a single exploration roll.
