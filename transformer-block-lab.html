Transformer Block makes one hidden mechanism visible.

Attention, FFN, residuals, and norm form one layer. This playground keeps the moving parts small so the calculation can be followed from input to output.

Read the playground

Move the control and watch the highlighted result change. The four stages describe the mechanism in the same order as the code.

What this isolates

Attention, FFN, residuals, and norm form one layer. The toy values are teaching data; real models use larger vectors, matrices, vocabularies, and datasets.

Why it matters

This mechanism is one link in the first-principles chain from text to tokens, vectors, attention, prediction, and learning.

Terms

input is what enters the operation. transform is the calculation. output is what the next mechanism receives.

A short history

The block assembled here was introduced in Vaswani and colleagues' Attention Is All You Need (2017). Its two supporting ideas are older: residual (skip) connections come from He and colleagues' ResNet work (2015), and layer normalization from Ba, Kiros, and Hinton (2016). The original paper normalized after each residual add; most modern LLMs such as GPT-2 onward normalize before each sublayer (pre-norm). This toy keeps the residual arithmetic and omits the norm step.
