Calculation numbers behind the arrows

Cosine ranges from −1 to 1. It is not a probability.

Vectors · dot product · cosine similarity

Different lengths. The same direction.

A vector has both length and direction. The dot product responds to both. Cosine similarity divides out the lengths, leaving a comparison of direction. Two arrows can have different coordinates and still score 1.

Read the playground

Both arrows start at zero. The first coordinate moves an endpoint horizontally; the second moves it vertically. Changing either coordinate can change both length and direction.

The gold arc marks the angle between the arrows. The readout and four stages update whenever you drag an endpoint or edit a number.

One comparison at a time

Multiply matching coordinates, add the products, then divide by both vector lengths.

a · b = aₓbₓ + aᵧbᵧ
|a| = √(aₓ² + aᵧ²)
cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|)

At 90°, the products cancel. Beyond 90°, the dot product and cosine become negative.

Same direction ≠ same vector

[2, 3] and [4, 6] are different vectors. The second is twice as long, but it points in exactly the same direction. Their cosine similarity is 1.

To preserve direction, multiply every coordinate by the same positive number. Changing just the second coordinate usually turns the arrow. Multiplying by a negative number reverses it.

What the top bar changes

Vector a / b edits coordinates directly. Same direction, 90° apart, and Opposite load examples. Reset restores the original pair.

The plot expands for larger coordinates. Inputs accept finite values from −10,000 to 10,000; incomplete or out-of-range input leaves the last valid plot in place.

The words on this page

vector
An ordered list of coordinates, drawn here as an arrow from zero.
magnitude · |a|
The arrow’s length.
dot product · a · b
A sum of coordinate products: |a||b| cos θ.
cosine similarity
Agreement in direction: 1 aligned, 0 perpendicular, −1 opposite.
zero vector
[0, 0] has no direction. Its cosine comparison and angle are undefined.

Try this

  1. Choose Same direction. The lengths differ, but cosine is 1.
  2. Set b to [2.5, 3.5], matching a. The dot product halves; cosine stays at 1.
  3. Change only b.y to 1. The arrow turns and cosine changes.
  4. Compare 90° apart and Opposite. Watch the products cancel, then become negative.
  5. Set a.x and a.y to zero. Length becomes zero; direction is undefined.
