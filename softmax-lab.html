LLM first principles · Softmax Playground

Softmax makes one hidden mechanism visible.

Raw scores become competing probabilities. This playground keeps the moving parts small so the calculation can be followed from input to output.

Read the playground

Move the control and watch the highlighted result change. The four stages describe the mechanism in the same order as the code.

What this isolates

Raw scores become competing probabilities. The toy values are teaching data; real models use larger vectors, matrices, vocabularies, and datasets.

Why it matters

This mechanism is one link in the first-principles chain from text to tokens, vectors, attention, prediction, and learning.

Terms

input is what enters the operation. transform is the calculation. output is what the next mechanism receives.

Try this

  1. Move the control slowly and narrate each stage.
  2. Press Example to jump to a contrasting state.
  3. Reset and reproduce the change from the beginning.

A short history

The name softmax was popularized in neural-network classification work by John S. Bridle around 1989–1990. His work connected exponential output scores with probability distributions over classes. The same mathematical shape is also related to the Boltzmann distribution in statistical mechanics. Read Bridle's 1990 paper ↗
