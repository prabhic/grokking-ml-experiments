Embedding dimensions · distributed representation · information loss

A word has one vector. That vector has many numbers.

In the previous lab, two coordinates located a word on a plane. Here, six coordinates describe it together. We can inspect those numbers as bars without trying to draw six perpendicular axes on a screen. A 768-dimensional embedding extends the same idea: a longer list of coordinates.

Read the playground

Each card compares the same coordinate in words A and B. Move a slider to edit A. The number at the bottom is that coordinate’s contribution to the dot product.

The full vector remains six-dimensional even when you omit coordinates from the comparison. The selected score uses only checked coordinates; the full score always uses all six.

Why more than two numbers?

Extra coordinates give a representation more ways to distinguish patterns. In our teaching table, cat and car match on D1 and D2. Their remaining coordinates distinguish them.

Press Same first two to load this example. Then press All 6. Nothing about the stored words changed; the comparison gained information. More dimensions alone do not guarantee useful embeddings—the values need to be learned well.

The whole pattern matters

D3 is not a “cat dimension.” We deliberately leave axes unnamed. In learned embeddings, meaning is generally spread across coordinates rather than assigned one tidy concept per coordinate.

Rotating every vector together can change all its coordinates while preserving dot products and cosine similarities. A single coordinate’s meaning therefore depends on the representation’s choice of axes.

One comparison at a time

dot = Σ AᵢBᵢ over included i
|A| = √(Σ Aᵢ² over included i)
cosine = dot / (|A| × |B|)

Omitting coordinates changes both the dot product and the lengths. A large positive product does not by itself determine the final similarity.

If no dimensions are included, or either selected vector is zero, cosine is undefined.

The words on this page

dimension
One coordinate position in every vector. All entries share the same coordinate system.
representation
The complete numeric pattern assigned to an item.
contribution
Aᵢ × Bᵢ, one term in the dot product. Opposite signs give a negative term.
selected subspace
The coordinates included in this comparison. Omitting coordinates can hide differences.
embedding width
The number of coordinates per entry; a model design choice, not the vocabulary size.

Try this

  1. Start with cat and dog. Most coordinate patterns resemble each other.
  2. Press Same first two. Selected cosine is 1 for cat and car, but their full vectors differ.
  3. Include D3. Watch its negative product enter the sum and the selected similarity fall.
  4. Press All 6. Edit one cat coordinate and follow its effect on the sum, lengths, and score.
  5. Uncheck all six dimensions. The result becomes undefined. Reset to restore the starting experiment.
